Getting Started with ExoDip

ExoDip provides rapid automated screening of astronomical photometric observations. The workflow follows a standard sequence:

  1. Data Acquisition: Obtain calibrated time-series flux measurements from space-borne missions (e.g. NASA Kepler, TESS) or ground-based surveys.
  2. Ingestion: In the Analyze tab, drag and drop your data file (CSV, TXT, or NPY) into the upload zone or select a pre-loaded benchmark row.
  3. Screening: Click Run Transit Screening. The pipeline detrends flux, runs Astropy Box Least Squares periodograms, extracts morphological metrics, and queries the classifier ensemble.
  4. Telemetry Review: Inspect the interactive full light curve, zoomed transit events, BLS signal-to-noise ratio, period, depth, and consensus scores.
  5. Export: Download complete machine-readable telemetry in JSON format using Export Telemetry (JSON).

End-to-End Processing Architecture

The ExoDip pipeline combines classical Fourier/periodogram methods with machine-learning classification:

  • Calibration & Normalization: Raw flux is baseline-subtracted and median-normalized: F_norm = (F - F_median) / F_std.
  • Trend Removal: Moving-window boxcar convolution preserves transit signatures while mitigating low-frequency instrumental drift.
  • Periodic Dip Finding: Astropy BLS evaluates trial box models across orbital periods from 0.5 to 20 days.
  • Feature Formulation: 15 morphological features are derived from the raw and smoothed profiles.
  • Screening Inference: Feature vectors are classified by tuned XGBoost, Random Forest, CNN, and SVM models.

Box Least Squares (BLS) Transit Search

The Box Least Squares (Kovács et al., 2002) algorithm models transits as periodic rectangular dips characterized by period P, duration q, epoch t_0, and transit depth δ:

ExoDip calculates the BLS periodogram over a fine frequency grid corresponding to periods from 0.5 to 20 days, testing fractional durations from 0.01 to 0.15 of the trial period. The peak periodogram power and its signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) quantify the significance of candidate periodic dips.

Extracted Astrophysical Feature Vectors

The 15 calculated features include:

  • Statistical Moments: Mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis, and negative-to-positive deviation ratio.
  • Signal Dynamics: Total signal energy (sum of squared deviations) and spectral Shannon entropy.
  • Transit Morphology: Average transit depth (ppm), duration (cadences), minimum flux, and distinct dip count.
  • BLS Parameters: Best-fit period, duration, transit SNR, and maximum periodogram power.

Model Architectures & Training Methodology

ExoDip evaluates candidates using an ensemble of four distinct supervised classifiers trained on validated Kepler mission light curves:

  • XGBoost (Primary Classifier): Gradient-boosted decision trees tuned for class-imbalance sensitivity and optimal composite cross-validation score (Accuracy: 0.9912, Recall: 1.0000).
  • Random Forest: 150-tree ensemble providing robust out-of-bag variance estimation and feature importance ranking.
  • Convolutional Neural Network (CNN): 1-D convolutional filter architecture capturing multi-scale local ingress/egress transit dip features.
  • Support Vector Machine (SVM): Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel classifier mapping non-linear boundary separations.

Interpreting Screening Results

  • Candidate vs Non-Candidate: Candidate status indicates that the light curve exhibits periodic box-like attenuations with high BLS SNR and classifier probability (≥ 50%).
  • Transit Depth: The percentage of stellar light obscured during transit: δ ≈ (R_p / R_*)^2. For a solar-radius star, 1% depth corresponds roughly to a Jupiter-sized planet (11 R_Earth); 0.01% corresponds to an Earth-sized planet (1 R_Earth).
  • BLS SNR: Values > 5.0 denote statistically significant candidate periodic events; values < 3.0 are generally indistinguishable from stellar noise.

Astrophysical Limitations & False Positives

Users must consider known astrophysical and observational false positives when vetting candidates:

  • Eclipsing Binaries (EBs): Grazing binary stars and secondary eclipses can produce U- or V-shaped dips mimicking exoplanet transits. Check for alternating eclipse depths or out-of-transit ellipsoidal variations.
  • Blended Background Stars: A faint eclipsing binary within the photometric aperture can produce shallow transit-depth dilution.
  • Stellar Variability: Spot modulation and rotational variation can create spurious periodicities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does ExoDip confirm exoplanets?
A: No. ExoDip is a screening and triage tool. Planetary confirmation strictly requires radial velocity spectroscopy (to measure planetary mass) and high-contrast imaging.

Q: Why is XGBoost used as the primary model?
A: Benchmark evaluation on 570 held-out Kepler light curves demonstrated that XGBoost achieved the highest composite score across precision, recall, and ROC-AUC.

Q: Can I analyze TESS 2-minute cadence light curves?
A: Yes. Export the flux column from Lightkurve or MAST into CSV format and upload it into ExoDip.

About ExoDip

ExoDip is an open-source astronomical candidate screening application engineered to assist researchers, educators, and astronomy students in analyzing stellar time-series observations for exoplanetary transits.

Combining Astropy Box Least Squares (BLS) period searching, astrophysical feature profiling, and gradient-boosted decision trees, ExoDip automates the initial triage and identification of candidate transit signals across space- and ground-based photometric surveys.
